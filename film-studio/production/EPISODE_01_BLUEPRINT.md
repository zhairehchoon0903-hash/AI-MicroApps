# THE LAST BLOODLINE — Episode 01 Blueprint

**Working title:** OPEN — do not lock without creator approval.  
**Status:** Story foundation based on the creator's established opening premise; exact names, dialogue, runtime, locations, and mechanics remain open.  
**Episode purpose:** Introduce the baby, establish immediate danger, show the Fairy's intervention, make the transfer to the human world emotionally consequential, and end with a strong forward-driving question.

## 1. Non-negotiable opening beats

1. Baby Auren is snatched from his original world.
2. A Fairy intervenes to help him.
3. The rescue attempt goes wrong.
4. The baby is transferred into the human world.
5. The story establishes that he will grow up as an orphan, be bullied, and display extraordinary powers even as a baby.
6. The wider Five Kingdoms and their factions will seek him for different reasons as he grows; the opening should plant mystery rather than explain every faction.

## 2. Dramatic intention

The audience should first care about the child, then fear for him, then feel the cost of the failed intervention. The opening is not a catalogue of fantasy species or a history lesson. It is a human emotional event inside a vast, dangerous world.

The sequence should leave the audience with three connected questions:
- Who took the child, and why?
- Why did the Fairy risk intervening?
- What does the child's arrival in the human world set in motion?

## 3. Sequence outline

### Sequence A — The taking
**Purpose:** Start with immediate danger and a clear emotional centre.

- Open on a visually striking, active moment. The child is taken; do not spend the opening minutes explaining the world.
- Show the baby's vulnerability through physical action and reaction: a reaching hand, a cry, a sudden silence, a protective instinct from someone nearby if that person is established.
- Keep the identity and motive of the abductors partially concealed unless the scene needs a clear antagonist.
- Use sound, spatial clarity, and urgency to make the abduction understandable in a vertical frame.
- Do not reveal the full bloodline history.

**End beat:** The child is beyond immediate protection; the Fairy recognizes the danger and acts.

### Sequence B — The Fairy intervenes
**Purpose:** Make the Fairy's choice emotionally legible.

- The Fairy enters the action with a clear objective: help the baby.
- Show the Fairy making a choice under pressure, not simply appearing to cast a spell.
- Let the audience read the risk through movement, expression, and the environment.
- Keep the exact relationship between Fairy and child unresolved until approved.
- Establish that the intervention has consequences and is not a guaranteed rescue.

**End beat:** The rescue mechanism destabilizes or is disrupted. The precise cause is OPEN and must be chosen before final scripting.

### Sequence C — The transfer goes wrong
**Purpose:** Turn a rescue attempt into a separation with lasting consequences.

- A magical or world-crossing event occurs, but its exact rules and visual language are OPEN.
- The child is transferred into the human world.
- The Fairy's attempt fails in a specific, causally understandable way. Avoid an arbitrary accident with no later meaning.
- The audience should understand what happened physically even if it does not yet understand why the worlds are connected.
- Use a strong emotional visual: the Fairy reaching for the child, the child's hand slipping away, or another approved action that communicates separation without explanatory dialogue.

**End beat:** The Fairy and child are separated. The child's fate is unknown to the people left behind.

### Sequence D — A child in the human world
**Purpose:** Shift from cosmic danger to intimate vulnerability.

- Establish the human world through a concrete location and grounded sensory details.
- Show the baby arriving and the immediate danger or uncertainty of being alone.
- A caretaker or discoverer may be introduced only after the creator approves the character and circumstances.
- Do not invent the identity of the person who finds or raises him.
- Make the contrast between the two worlds visible through production design, sound, lighting, and scale rather than a speech.

**End beat:** The child survives, but his origin remains hidden.

### Sequence E — A glimpse of the life ahead
**Purpose:** Connect the opening event to the orphaned, bullied child and his extraordinary early powers without rushing through his whole childhood.

Possible structure (PROPOSED; select only after creator approval):
- A short, carefully chosen time transition.
- A glimpse of Auren as a small child experiencing exclusion or bullying.
- An unexplained power manifestation that protects, exposes, or frightens him.
- A quiet emotional beat showing the cost of being different.

Do not montage through every life stage. The audience needs time to feel the child’s experience.

**End beat / cliffhanger options (PROPOSED):**
- An unexplained sign suggests that someone in the original world has detected the child.
- A faction receives evidence that the child survived.
- Auren's power produces a symbol or effect recognized by someone who should not know he exists.
- A danger approaches the human world, but its identity is withheld.

Choose one cliffhanger that follows logically from the final approved plot mechanics. Do not use all options.

## 4. Character and performance direction

### The baby
The child cannot deliver exposition. Use gaze, crying, reaching, startled reactions, breath, small movements, and the way adults respond to him. The audience's connection should come from his vulnerability and the extraordinary events surrounding him.

### The Fairy
The Fairy must have a clear reason to intervene and a readable emotional response when the transfer goes wrong. Do not over-explain the Fairy's history in the first episode.

### The abductors
Their identities and motives remain open. Their actions should be clear enough to follow while their deeper purpose can remain mysterious.

## 5. Visual and sound approach

- Start in motion, not with a long title card or static exposition.
- Establish geography through wide-to-medium-to-close coverage, adapted to the 9:16 frame.
- Use close-ups for emotional recognition, not as the only visual language.
- Use motivated camera movement during pursuit, intervention, and transfer.
- Keep the baby's face and the Fairy's hands/eyes visible during the crucial separation beat.
- Give the magical event a consistent visual signature once the magic system is approved.
- Use sound to distinguish the worlds and intensify the failed rescue: environment, breath, cloth, footsteps, magical resonance, sudden dropouts, and aftermath.
- Do not use music to tell the audience what it should feel in every moment. Allow some moments to breathe.

## 6. Shot-planning template

For every shot, record:
- Shot ID / sequence
- Duration
- Character(s) and approved reference image IDs
- Location and time
- Framing / lens impression
- Camera movement
- On-screen action (one principal action per shot)
- Facial performance / emotional beat
- Dialogue (if any)
- Sound effects / ambience / music cue
- Lighting / weather
- Continuity dependencies
- Generation prompt
- Negative constraints
- Estimated generation cost
- Review status

## 7. Questions to resolve before final screenplay lock

1. What is the baby's confirmed full name and surname?
2. Who takes the baby, and what do they want?
3. Who is the Fairy, and why does the Fairy intervene?
4. What specifically goes wrong during the intervention?
5. Is the transfer accidental, redirected, sabotaged, or the result of a deliberate sacrifice?
6. Where in the human world does the baby arrive?
7. Who discovers him, and how does he become an orphan?
8. How much of the first episode takes place during infancy versus childhood?
9. What is the first visible power manifestation, and what does it cost or risk?
10. What is the first cliffhanger, and what question should it leave the audience asking?

These questions are a production gate, not a demand to answer everything immediately. Build the screenplay around confirmed beats and leave other details open until chosen.
