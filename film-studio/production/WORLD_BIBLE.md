# THE LAST BLOODLINE — World Bible

**Status:** Foundational framework. Names, geography, history, political systems, and detailed lore remain open unless explicitly marked confirmed.

## 1. Worldbuilding rule

The setting must feel like a functioning world that existed before Auren's arrival and continues beyond the camera's view. Do not build lore merely to decorate a scene. Every important place, people, faction, custom, and magical rule should affect a character's choices or the story's consequences.

Do not silently assign names, rulers, borders, histories, or motives to the Five Kingdoms. This document provides a structured place to develop them with the creator.

## 3. Peoples and civilizations

The following peoples or groups have been mentioned in the project world. Their detailed nature and relationship to the kingdoms remain OPEN:
- Fairies
- Dragons / Dragon-kind
- Elves
- Gnomes
- Tribes (specific identities not yet established)

For each people, decide:
- Are they a species, culture, nation, order, or broad family of peoples?
- Where do they live?
- What do they value and fear?
- How do they organize families, leadership, and community?
- What are their abilities and limitations?
- What is their relationship to magic?
- What do they think of humans and the Five Kingdoms?
- What internal disagreements exist?
- What is their connection, if any, to Auren?

Avoid treating an entire people as morally identical. Give individuals different beliefs, loyalties, and choices.

## 4. The Void — OPEN

The Void has been mentioned as part of the story's fantasy vocabulary, but its exact nature is not established in this bible.

Decisions to make:
- Is it a place, dimension, force, condition, civilization, or name used by different cultures?
- Is it sentient or governed?
- What can enter or leave it?
- What are its rules and limits?
- How does it relate to the ancient enemy and Auren's bloodline?
- What can the audience learn early without revealing the central mystery?
- What is the true explanation, and at which story stage should it be revealed?

Do not equate the Void with the ancient enemy unless that connection is explicitly approved.

## 5. Magic system — framework, not final rules

Magic must be consistent enough that the audience can understand risk and consequence. Its detailed source and laws remain OPEN.

Every power or magical practice should specify:
1. Source: where the ability comes from.
2. Access: who can use it and how.
3. Trigger: what activates it.
4. Effect: what it visibly and practically does.
5. Range and duration.
6. Limits: what it cannot do.
7. Cost: physical, mental, social, moral, or material consequence.
8. Failure: what happens when it is interrupted or misused.
9. Training: what skill or discipline is required.
10. Counterplay: how another character might resist or respond.

### Auren's magic
Confirmed only at the broad level: Auren displays extraordinary powers even as a baby. Exact abilities, source, visual effects, costs, limits, and relationship to his bloodline remain OPEN.

His magic must complement his swordsmanship as he matures. Do not let magical spectacle erase the craft, discipline, and tactical intelligence of sword combat.

## 6. Ancient history and bloodline mystery

The story contains a bloodline mystery and an ancient threat or enemy in the developing concept. Their full history and exact relationship are OPEN.

Build a private chronology before revealing it to the audience:
- What actually happened in the ancient past?
- Which sources are reliable, mistaken, biased, or deliberately deceptive?
- What was lost, hidden, rewritten, or forgotten?
- Why does Auren matter now?
- Why do different factions interpret his existence differently?
- Which evidence appears in each episode?
- What is the final truth, and what emotional cost comes with learning it?

Do not write the opening as a lore lecture. Let the audience experience the consequences first.

## 7. Geography and travel

Map the world before staging long journeys. Track:
- Relative positions of kingdoms and territories
- Natural barriers and routes
- Travel time by foot, horse, ship, flight, or magic
- Climate and seasonal changes
- Borders, disputed areas, and neutral ground
- Locations where travel is dangerous or impossible

A character must not arrive somewhere instantly unless a previously established ability or device explains it.

## 8. Political and faction logic

For every faction, record:
- Leadership and internal hierarchy
- Public doctrine and private agenda
- Resources and capabilities
- What it knows about Auren
- What it believes about Auren
- What it wants from him
- What it fears will happen if another faction reaches him first
- Limits it will not cross
- Internal dissent and competing plans

The Five Kingdoms should not all pursue identical goals. Their different interpretations of the same evidence can drive conflict.

## 10. World continuity rules

- A location retains its geography and visual identity between scenes.
- Travel time, weather, and time of day must be tracked.
- Political borders and allegiances do not change without a story event.
- Magic follows established rules; new exceptions require explicit explanation.
- Cultures should not change their customs merely to serve a scene.
- Rumours, myths, and propaganda must be distinguished from objective story truth.
- A character may be wrong about the world; the production bible must track what is actually true.
